# Archival Record: History of Education in Ethiopia

## 1. Classical Indigenous Ecclesiastical Education (4th Century – Present)
Ethiopia possesses one of the oldest continuous literate and pedagogical traditions in Africa. Following the adoption of Christianity in the Kingdom of Axum around 330 CE under King Ezana and Bishop Frumentius (Abba Salama Kasate Berhan), the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church developed an integrated multi-tiered collegiate educational system.

### Tier 1: Nebab Bet (School of Reading)
- **Entrance Age:** Typically 4 to 7 years.
- **Pedagogy:** Rote syllabic learning of the Ge'ez script (*Fidel*), consisting of 26 base consonant families and 7 vocalic modifications (>200 unique graphemes).
- **Textual Sequence:**
  1. *Fidel Hawaria*: Letter mastery and phonetic articulation.
  2. *Melikte Hawariat*: The Epistles of the Apostles.
  3. *Wengel*: The Four Gospels.
  4. *Dawit*: The 150 Psalms of David. Completion of *Dawit* signified functional literacy, conferring the title of *Yeqolo Temari* (wandering student).

### Tier 2: Zema Bet (School of Liturgical Music & Sacred Dance)
- **Origin:** Established in the 6th century by Saint Yared of Axum.
- **Notation System:** Indigenous 10-symbol neum-like notation (*Seraye*) mapped across three melodic modes (*Ge'ez*, *Ezel*, *Araray*).
- **Graduation Volumes:** *Deggwa* (Great Antiphonary), *Tsome Deggwa* (Lenten Hymns), *Me'eraf*, *Zimare*, and *Mewasit*.
- **Sacred Dance (*Aqqawaqam*):** Choreographed spiritual rhythm utilizing the *Senasel* (sistrum), *Kebero* (sacred drum), and *Meqomiya* (prayer staff).

### Tier 3: Qene Bet (School of Poetry & Philosophy)
- **Curriculum:** Ge'ez classical grammar (*Sewasew*), dialectics, and spontaneous metrical verse composition.
- **The Wax and Gold Principle (*Sem-enna-Worq*):**
  - *Sem* (The Wax): Surface-level poetic rhyme and apparent literal narrative.
  - *Worq* (The Gold): The veiled metaphysical, political, or philosophical reality.
- **Metrical Forms:** *Gubae Qana*, *Ze-Amlakiye*, *Mibezu*, *Wazema*, *Selast*, *Mewedis*, and *Kebre Ye'eti*.
- **Philosophical Zenith:** Produced rationalist philosopher Zera Yacob (1599–1692), author of the *Hatata*, pioneering rational inquiry prior to European Enlightenment.

### Tier 4: Metsahaf Bet (School of Canonical Books & Jurisprudence)
- **Divisions:**
  - *Beluy* (Old Testament Exegesis).
  - *Haddis* (New Testament Hermeneutics).
  - *Liqawunt* (Patristic Jurisprudence & Canon Law).
  - *Menekosat* (Monastic literature & ethics).
- **Core Secular & Civil Code:** *Fetha Negest* (The Law of the Kings), dictating civil contracts, inheritance, penal sanctions, and royal governance.

## 2. Traditional Islamic Education (7th Century – Present)
- **Historical Hubs:** Harar Jugol (UNESCO World Heritage Site), Wollo, Jimma, and the Gurage highlands.
- **Levels:**
  1. *Quranic Madrasa*: Memorization (*Tahfiz*) and phonetic Arabic calligraphy (*Kitabah*).
  2. *Ilm (Higher Knowledge)*: Arabic syntax (*Nahw*), morphology (*Sarf*), and Hadith studies.
  3. *Fiqh*: Islamic jurisprudence according to the Shafi'i and Hanafi legal traditions.
- **Literary Artifacts:** Extensive preservation of Ajami manuscripts (local Ethiopian languages written in adapted Arabic script).

## 3. The Modern Era: State Schools & Higher Education
- **1908:** Emperor Menelik II opens the **Menelik II School** in Addis Ababa, introducing modern curricula (French, English, Mathematics, Sciences).
- **1925:** Regent Ras Teferi (Emperor Haile Selassie I) inaugurates the **Teferi Mekonnen School**.
- **1931:** Empress Menen Asfaw establishes the **Empress Menen Girls' School**, institutionalizing modern female secondary education.
- **1950:** The **University College of Addis Ababa (UCAA)** is founded under Dr. Lucien Matte, later chartered as **Haile Selassie I University (HSIU)** in 1961 (now **Addis Ababa University - AAU**).
- **1974–1991:** The National Literacy Campaign (*Edget Behibret*) mobilizes over 60,000 university students to rural kebeles, drastically raising literacy and winning the UNESCO Krupskaya Award in 1980.
- **2000s–Present:** Tertiary expansion to **50+ public universities** across all regional states, implementing the 70:30 national STEM ratio and establishing modern research institutes in artificial intelligence and biotechnology.
