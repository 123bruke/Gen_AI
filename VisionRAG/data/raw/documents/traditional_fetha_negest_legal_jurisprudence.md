# Jurisprudential Treatise: The Fetha Negest (Law of the Kings)

## Origin & Codification
The *Fetha Negest* (Ge'ez: ፍትሐ ነገሥት, "The Law of the Kings") is the monumental socio-legal and constitutional foundation of the Ethiopian Empire. Originally synthesized in 13th-century Coptic Egypt by Christian jurist al-Safi ibn al-Assal, it was translated into Ge'ez during the reign of Emperor Zer'a Ya'eqob (1434–1468) and became the supreme legal curriculum taught in the *Metsahaf Bet*.

## Structure of the Code
The *Fetha Negest* is organized into two primary books containing 51 canonical discourses:
1. **Part One: Ecclesiastical Jurisprudence (Chapters 1–22):**
   - The hierarchy of the patriarchate, bishops, presbyters, and deacons.
   - Sacramental legitimacy, baptism, matrimony, and monastic vows.
   - Administration of church tithes, sanctuaries, and scriptural libraries.
2. **Part Two: Civil and Penal Jurisprudence (Chapters 23–51):**
   - **Civil Law:** Contracts of sale, loans, usury prohibitions, pledges, partnerships (*Hibrete Simret*), and real estate boundary demarcation.
   - **Family Law:** Marriage contracts, dowries, grounds for separation, child custody, and legitimate inheritance shares.
   - **Criminal Law:** Homicide distinctions (intentional vs. involuntary manslaughter), compensation (*Dega* or blood price), assault, theft restitution, and perjury penalties.
   - **Constitutional Governance (Chapter 44):** The duties of the Christian monarch (*Negusa Nagast*), limits of sovereign authority, judicial impartiality, and rules of lawful warfare.

## Impact on Modern Ethiopian Law
The *Fetha Negest* remained the primary legal foundation of the Ethiopian Empire until Emperor Haile Selassie promulgated the 1930 Constitution and the modern Civil and Penal Codes of 1957–1960 crafted in collaboration with French jurist René David. Concepts of equity, customary land tenancy, and arbitration rooted in the *Fetha Negest* continue to inform modern Ethiopian jurisprudence.
